## Overview

Several [airports near NYC](/guides/best-ga-airports-near-nyc/) have flight schools and flying clubs that allow for renting aircraft.

Joining a flying club and renting from a flight school differ in a few important ways:

- A club usually charges "wet tach" rather than "wet Hobbs", which results in significantly cheaper flight hours.
- Clubs (including Richmond Pilots) allow for multi-day trips with advance planning.
- Flight schools have no upfront cost, but significantly higher hourly aircraft rates.
- Depending on the school, there may be more options for types of aircraft and better availability for instructors.

## Paying tach vs. Hobbs

Flight schools around NYC typically charge a "wet Hobbs" rate. Wet rates include fuel, and the Hobbs meter is a clock that runs whenever the engine is running.

In contrast, most flying clubs charge a "wet tach" rate. Similarly, fuel is included, but the tach meter counts engine revolutions and runs more slowly at lower RPMs, such as during taxi and descent.

On a typical flight in the club's aircraft, the tach records about {{< tach-ratio >}} of the time the Hobbs does. The gap is biggest on lessons and pattern work, with lots of time at low power, and smallest on a long cruise. Around NYC, time spent taxiing and waiting for takeoff adds up quickly, and on a Hobbs rate you pay for all of it.

## Multi-day trips

Few flight schools will allow for multi-day trips using their aircraft. At flying clubs like Richmond Pilots, it's the norm. Our members often take the plane for the weekend to places like North Carolina, Martha's Vineyard, or AirVenture in Oshkosh, Wisconsin.

{{< photo src="img/camping.jpg" alt="Club Cessna 172M N5272R parked on the grass with tents under its wings at EAA AirVenture in Oshkosh, Wisconsin" caption="N5272R, camping under the wing at AirVenture, Oshkosh · Photo: Dave Gramatges" >}}

Flying clubs handle multi-day trips differently, but at Richmond Pilots there is no daily minimum and you only pay for the hours you actually fly (e.g., if the plane sits for two days, you do not pay extra for that).

## Flight school pros

As an honest counterpoint, there are lots of good reasons to rent from flight schools:

- **No waitlists**: Flying clubs typically have waitlists that rely on members leaving. Sometimes the waitlist is empty; other times it can take several months.
- **No upfront fee**: Part 61 flight schools have no admission fee, deposit, or monthly dues.
- **More variety**: Flight schools may have a larger fleet and a wider variety of aircraft to fly.
- **More instructors**: Richmond Pilots has club CFIs, but flight schools are likely to have a larger number of CFIs available.
- **Less responsibility**: No club meetings, plane washes, or additional costs.

## Flying club benefits aside from cost and availability

Flying clubs are a great way to join a group of pilots to co-own aircraft. As a result, you meet new people to fly with and gain a deeper understanding of aircraft maintenance.

Aircraft availability is usually higher with flying clubs because the membership is fixed.

Our monthly meetings are a great way to stay informed about aircraft maintenance, club finances, and other ongoing matters, and to keep learning through safety presentations from the chief pilot.
