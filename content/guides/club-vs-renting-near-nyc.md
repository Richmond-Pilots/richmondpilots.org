Joining a flying club and renting from a flight school differ in a few important ways:

- A club usually charges "wet tach" versus "wet hobbs", which results in significantly cheaper flight hours.
- Clubs (including Richmond Pilots) allow for multi-day trips with advanced planning.
- Flight schools have no upfront cost, but signficiantly higher hourly aircraft rates.
- Depending on the school, there may be more options for types of aircraft and better availability for instructors.

## Paying Tach vs. Hobbs

Flight schools around NYC will typically charge "wet hobbs" rate. Wet rates include fuel, and the hobbs meter is a clock that runs whenever the master switch is on.

In contrast, most flying clubs charge "wet tach" rate. Similarly, fuel is included, but the tach meter counts engine revolutions and runs more slowly at lower RPMs like during taxi, descent, etc.

The gap is biggest on lessons and pattern work, with lots of time at low power, and smallest on a long cruise. Around NYC, time spent taxiing and waiting for takeoff adds up quickly, and on a Hobbs rate you pay for all of it.

## Multi-day trips

Few flight schools will allow for multi-day trips using their aircraft. At flying clubs like Richmond Pilots that's part of the norm. Our members often take the plane for the weekend to places like North Carolina, Martha's Vineyard, or to AirVenture in Oshkosh, Wisconsin.

{{< photo src="img/camping.jpg" alt="Club Cessna 172M N5272R parked on the grass with tents under its wings at EAA AirVenture in Oshkosh, Wisconsin" caption="N5272R, camping under the wing at AirVenture, Oshkosh · Photo: Dave Gramatges" >}}

Flying clubs handle multi-day trips differently, but at Richmond Pilots there is no daily minimum and you only pay for the hours you actually fly (e.g. if the plane sits for 2 days, you do not pay extra for that).

## Flying School Pros

As an honest counterpoint, there are lots of good reasons to rent from flying schools:

- **No waitlists**: Flying clubs typically have waitlists that rely on members leaving. Sometimes the waitlist is empty, other times it could take several months.
- **No upfront fee**: Part 61 flying schools have no admission fee, deposit, or monthly dues.
- **More variety**: Flying schools may have a larger fleet and more variety of aircraft to fly.
- **More instructors**: Richmond Pilots has club CFIs, but flying schools are likely to have a larger number of CFIs available.
- **Less responsibility**: No club meetings, plane washes, or additional costs.

## Flying Club benefits aside from cost and availability

Flying clubs are a great way to join a group of pilots to co-own aircraft together. As a result, you meet new people to fly with, and more deeply learn and understand aircraft maintenance.

Generally speaking, aircraft availability is usually higher with flying clubs because the membership is fixed.

Our monthly meetings are a great way to understand the aircraft maintenance, club finances, ongoing considerations, and continue learning via safety presentations from the chief pilot.
